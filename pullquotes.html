Pull quotes that earn the interruption

Six ways to pull a line out of an article 1 story, each shown in place at real size. They draw on what L.A. TACO’s streets already do with lettering: painted walls, LED storefront signs, stickers on the back of a taco truck.

Why change itThe red box reads like an alert or an ad, it fights the red masthead and the More Opinion fascia, and the mustard rule is the default every CMS ships.

A

Hanging quote marks

No box. A giant chromatic Sutro Deluxe quote mark hangs out into the margin, like the first letter on a painted sign. The quote sits in the text column at 40px. (Chosen for article 1, without the underline.)

Use it forThe everyday default, any section, any length up to about 30 words.

CostLow. One component, no images. Works in the 680 column and on mobile.

Not long after my close call, I was sent to cover a drug bust in a national forest. Federal officers were removing hundreds of pounds of marijuana, and my correspondent arranged for me to fly with them as they removed the plants.

Two weeks ago, Los Angeles lost more precious lives when NBC4 and Telemundo 52’s news chopper tragically fell to the ground and erupted in flames.

B

Rótulo wall

The quote gets painted on a wall. It breaks out of the grid to the full width of the window, in the section’s wall colour (Opinion is Dodger blue, as in article 2), with stacked lines sized to fill the wall and a sign painter’s double pinstripe around it.

Use it forOnce per story, on the line the story turns on. Short quotes only, 6 to 14 words.

CostMedium. Needs a line-break field in the CMS (or fit-to-width JS) and a colour per section.

In 1977, Francis Gary Powers and George Spears were killed in the tragic crash of KNBC’s “telecopter.” In 2007, a pair of Arizona news choppers collided while covering a pursuit, killing all four occupants.

I was forced to make that decision years ago as a participant in this Russian Roulette of daily broadcasting. I felt my days were being numbered.

C

LED storefront sign

The scrolling red LED sign from every swap meet, liquor store and taquería window (ABIERTO, TACOS, CHECKS CASHED). The quote crawls across a dot-matrix panel at the full story width. This is the page’s one moving moment, and it stops on hover. With reduced motion it sits still and wraps.

Use it forNews and breaking stories, quotes that work as a slogan. Under 12 words.

CostMedium. Pure CSS. Figma shows one static frame of it.

The news chopper community will decry my query, but we have other options. Police departments have embraced drone technology to address crime, reducing helicopter-related danger.

We can still watch aerials of chickens roaming the streets and the inevitable high-speed car chase that viewers have come to love.

D

Slap sticker

The die-cut sticker you find on the back window of a taco truck: cream vinyl with a white kiss-cut edge, slapped on at an angle, cutting into the column so the text wraps around it, and sticking out into the gutter. A red starburst from the homepage guide cards stamps it “LA NETA” (the honest truth).

Use it forFunny, sharp or very human lines. Food and culture stories. Under 20 words.

CostLow. Float plus a rotation. On mobile it sits between paragraphs, still tilted.

NETA

“I pitched it, three-pointer style, into the trash bin.”

Scott Shulman, on his Golden Mike award

Within a year, my participation was rewarded with a “Golden Mike” award for best coverage of a live breaking story. I didn’t attend the ceremony; I was already living with a strange sense of survivor’s guilt. I couldn’t fathom being rewarded for capturing the death of an armed criminal live when all I did was point the camera.

When UPS delivered my Golden Mike, I pitched it—three-pointer style—into the trash bin. Since Stroh’s death, the passion for live news televised from noisy helicopters has proliferated the airwaves. Fires, floods, O.J. Simpson’s “Bronco Blanco” chase, and yes, the staple of aerial news, the high-speed police pursuit.

E

Marker in the running text

The line stays where it is, so nothing repeats. A hand-pulled mustard marker stroke picks it out, and a note in the gutter (where a reader would scribble) offers to share just that sentence. Quieter than the others, and it can appear more than once.

Use it forNews, explainers and data stories, where a repeated quote feels like padding.

CostLow. A mark tag in the CMS. The gutter note needs the right rail to start lower.

I remember it as though it was yesterday, because I was the cameraman who shot it from the KABC helicopter. What I saw through my viewfinder was witnessed by tens of thousands of viewers, a violent death broadcast in real time.

F

Subtitle on the photo

The quote is subtitled onto an inline photo, like a still from a Spanish-dubbed movie on Channel 34: mustard Sutro with a comal outline, sitting low and centered. The photo and the line read as one moment.

Use it forProfiles and first-person stories, where the photo shows the person speaking. Never over food.

CostLow. Needs photos with a calm lower third, and a fallback to A when there isn’t one.

Sometimes we responded to a call on the police scanner; other times we flew to look at girls in bikinis on the Malibu beaches. Heck, it wasn’t illegal, and it was “fun.”

One of our regular pilots was a police officer who never got tired of buzzing his ex-girlfriend’s suburban home.
